# Copy

Every user-visible word: the interface, the README, the site, the post.

## The rule

Say what the reader gets.
Never spend a line on what will not happen.

"Search and preview work offline. Answers need the network."
Not "Chat is disabled in offline mode."

The second sentence spends its only line on an absence and leaves the reader to work out what remains.

## Do not restate the surface

If the panel prints the number, the paragraph does not explain that a number exists.
A line that repeats what is already on screen teaches the reader to skip the next one, and the next one is the one that mattered.

The index screen shows `12,481 files, 3,204 pages embedded, 241 MB`.
It does not add "Here you can see statistics about your index."

## Name the deciding fact

Be explicit about the one thing the reader could not have guessed and would want before saying yes.
Everything else is context.

For this product that fact is: nothing leaves the machine except the answer call, and the answer call is labeled every time.
That belongs high, stated plainly, once.

## Do not narrate restraint

"That is a capacity rule, not a sales tactic" is a sales tactic.
"We do not track you" invites the reader to wonder who does.

State the positive fact and stop.
"The index lives in one folder on this Mac."

## Do not answer an objection nobody raised

The denial plants the doubt.
A free local search tool does not need a paragraph about why it is free.

Anything the format already promises stays implicit.

## When a negation is allowed

Keep it only when it teaches a distinction and is followed by the right answer.

"Spotlight ranks. It cannot show you why. This shows you the patch that matched."
The negation is doing work: it separates two things a reader would otherwise conflate, and the sentence after it lands the point.

## Errors

An error message has two jobs: what happened, and the one action that fixes it.

Good: "Downloads is not readable. Grant access in System Settings, Privacy and Security, Files and Folders."
Bad: "Error: EACCES. Failed to read directory."

Never blame the user, never apologize, never say "unexpected".
Every error the code can produce has a written message. A raw exception reaching a person is a missing message, not an edge case.

## Skip reasons

The index screen lists skipped files, and each reason says what the file is, not that something failed.

"Image is 48 x 48, below the 300 px minimum."
"PDF is password protected."
"File is 340 MB, above the 200 MB limit."

A person reading that list is deciding whether the index is trustworthy.
Every reason they can understand builds that trust, and every "unsupported" spends it.

## Mechanics

Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings.
Buttons are verbs: "Add folder", "Rescan", "Forget file". Never "Submit", never "OK" where a real verb fits.
Numbers are digits. Units are spelled where they are read once and abbreviated where they sit in a column.
No em dashes. A plain dash, a comma, or two sentences.
No exclamation marks.
Second person for what the reader does, never first person plural for what the software does. The app is not a team.

## Spoken words

Anything a person will say out loud, the launch video included, is not caption text.
It is written as connected casual speech with contractions, delivered as prose to read aloud, never as a stack of one-clause fragments.
`LAUNCH.md` holds the script and follows this rule.
